# 第一章｜我們為何創造

**核心問題：** 人為什麼創造能夠擴展自身能力並改變世界的事物？

**獨有思想內容：**

- 人不只是適應世界，也透過創造改變自身與世界的關係。
- 創造源於需求，也源於好奇、想像、探索、表達與合作。
- 工具、機器與技術將人的能力延伸到身體之外。
- 創造使原本不存在的可能性進入現實。
- 人的創造不僅改變能做什麼，也可能改變人的活動方式。

**本章思想成果：** 建立 Creation｜創造作為人類擴展可能性、改變世界的基本活動。

**概念邊界：** 不在本章完整判斷創造的價值，不研究具體裝置的物理存在，也不提前展開責任理論。

### 英文版

**Why do we create?**

Human beings have filled the world with creations that were once beyond imagination. We have built places to live, crossed oceans, learned to fly, composed music, and developed ways of understanding things that our senses cannot directly perceive. Today, artificial intelligence allows us to create systems with capabilities that once required immediate human direction. Yet the history of creation cannot be understood through growing capability alone. **Through creation, we bring new possibilities into the world. Yet the significance of what we create lies not only in its capabilities, but also in what it makes possible for life.**

The things we make become part of places and relationships that existed before them. They can extend human action, preserve knowledge, invite exploration, and alter the conditions of everyday life. Some demand our attention; others serve us best when they allow us to turn our attention elsewhere. We create as people who have hopes and abilities, but also as inhabitants of a world we did not make and cannot claim for ourselves alone. To ask why we create is therefore to ask what draws us beyond the present, what creation brings into existence, and how the lives unfolding around it may be changed.

### 繁體中文版

**我們為何創造？**

人類在世界中留下了無數曾經難以想像的創造。我們建造居所、跨越海洋、學會飛行、創作音樂，也發展出理解感官無法直接察覺之事物的方法。今天，人工智能使我們能夠創造具備某些過去需要人直接指揮才能實現之能力的系統。然而，創造的歷史不能只從能力的增長來理解。**透過創造，我們使新的可能性進入世界。然而，創造的意義不只在於造物具有什麼能力，也在於它使生命與生活獲得什麼可能性。**

我們的造物進入的是早已存在的場所與關係之中。它們可以延伸人的行動、保存知識、開啟探索，也能改變日常生活的條件。有些造物需要我們持續關注，另一些則在使我們能夠將注意力留給其他事情時，發揮了最好的作用。我們以擁有願望與能力的人之身分創造，卻也始終生活於一個並非由自己創造、也不能獨自佔有的世界。追問我們為何創造，因此也需要理解：是什麼使人超越眼前，創造究竟使什麼成為現實，以及周圍的生命與生活如何因此改變。

## 1.1 Beyond What Is｜超越既有

### 英文版

A person can live within existing conditions without regarding those conditions as the end of all possibility. Familiar places, relationships, and routines give our lives continuity, but they do not define everything we may become. We can recognize the realities of our circumstances and still imagine experiences we have never had. The present gives our aspirations a place to begin, without determining how far they may reach. **To aspire is to recognize that the life we know is not the limit of the life we can imagine.** This capacity to see beyond what is already familiar allows people to question whether a different life might be possible.

We can cherish the lives we have while imagining another way of living. A person may love a familiar home and still wish to explore distant places. A community may value its traditions while finding new ways for its members to learn and gather. These desires need not arise from disappointment with what already exists. Sometimes it is precisely because a life is rich in memory and affection that people wish to carry its possibilities further. The wish for something new does not require everything old to be abandoned.

Not every aspiration begins with something missing. Curiosity, love, and wonder can lead us toward things whose value we have not yet learned to explain. People have observed distant stars without knowing whether their observations would lead to useful discoveries. A musician may search for a sound that has never been heard, moved by an experience that ordinary language cannot fully express. Such pursuits need not begin with a problem waiting to be solved, nor must their importance depend on an eventual practical result. Human aspiration includes the desire to encounter and understand what is unfamiliar, even when its significance remains unknown.

When we begin to imagine another way of living, what once seemed like the only possible arrangement can become one possibility among many. A task performed in the same way for generations may appear inseparable from the work itself. A familiar social practice can seem inevitable because its alternatives have rarely been considered. Imagination creates room to distinguish what is necessary from what has merely become customary. This does not make every alternative better or more desirable. It means that habit need no longer be mistaken for the full extent of what can be chosen.

Aspiration allows us to see beyond the present, but it does not mean every desire must be fulfilled. What we wish for can change as we learn more about ourselves, other people, and the conditions in which our wishes would take shape. A once compelling ambition may become less important when we understand what pursuing it would require. A possibility we had overlooked may come to matter more than the one we first desired. The freedom to aspire includes the freedom not to obey every aspiration. We can reconsider, reshape, or even relinquish what we once wished for.

Creation first begins not with technical capability, but with our ability to imagine possibilities beyond the life we already know. Yet imagination alone does not determine which possibilities we will pursue. Someone may envision a new instrument, a different place to live, or a way for people to work together, and still decide that the idea should remain unrealized. The movement toward creation begins when a person chooses to give an aspiration time, effort, and a place among other commitments. Even before the first object is made, that choice reveals what someone is willing to bring into the world. In this sense, the beginning of creation is not merely the discovery of possibility, but a decision to pursue one possibility rather than another.

### 繁體中文版

一個人可以生活在既有條件中，同時又不把那些條件視為一切可能性的終點。熟悉的場所、關係與日常習慣，使我們的生活具有延續性，卻不因此決定我們未來能夠成為什麼。我們可以認識自身處境的現實，同時想像尚未經歷的事物。當下為嚮往提供了起點，卻不決定它所能抵達的範圍。**有所嚮往，意味著人能夠認識到：眼前所知的生活，並不是想像所能抵達的全部生活。** 正是這種超越熟悉事物的能力，使人開始追問另一種生活是否可能。

人可以珍惜現有生活，卻仍然想像另一種生活；可以不否定現在，卻希望實現尚未存在的事物。一個人可能喜愛熟悉的家園，同時希望探索遙遠的地方；一個社區也可以珍惜傳統，並為成員尋找新的學習與相聚方式。這些願望不必源於對現狀的不滿。有時，正因為一段生活充滿記憶與情感，人們才希望把其中的美好延伸得更遠。嚮往新的事物，並不要求已有的一切都被放棄。

並非所有願望都始於欠缺。好奇、熱愛與驚奇，也能使人嚮往那些尚無法說明其價值的事物。人們可能觀察遙遠的星辰，卻不知道這些觀察是否終將帶來有用的發現。音樂家也可能受到某種難以用語言充分表達的經驗觸動，因而尋找從未有人聽過的聲音。這些追求不必始於等待解決的問題，其重要性也不一定取決於日後是否產生實際成果。人的嚮往還包括接觸與理解陌生事物的渴望，即使它們的意義尚未被人認識。

當人開始想像另一種生活，原本看似唯一的生活方式，便可能成為許多選擇中的一種。一項世代沿用的工作方法，可能被認為與工作本身不可分離；一種熟悉的社會習慣，也可能只是因為很少有人考慮其他方式，便被視為不可避免。想像使人有機會區分哪些事情確有必要，哪些只是長期形成的慣例。這並不使所有替代方案自動變得更好或更值得追求，而是讓習慣不再被誤認為選擇的全部範圍。

願望使人能夠超越眼前，卻不意味著每一個願望都必須得到滿足。隨著我們更了解自己、他人，以及願望將要實現的條件，原本希望追求的事物也可能改變。曾經令人熱切嚮往的目標，當我們理解實現它需要付出什麼，可能便不再那麼重要。另一種原本被忽略的可能性，也可能逐漸比最初的願望更值得珍惜。嚮往的自由，同時包括不必服從每一個願望的自由。人能夠重新理解、調整，甚至放棄曾經渴望的事物。

創造首先不是一種技術能力，而是人能夠想像現狀之外的可能性。然而，想像本身並不決定我們將追求哪一種可能。一個人可以設想新的儀器、不同的居所，或另一種合作方式，卻仍然決定不將構想付諸實現。當人願意為一項嚮往投入時間與心力，並在其他生活追求之間為它留下位置，創造才開始由願望走向行動。即使第一件造物尚未出現，這種選擇也已經顯示了人希望將什麼帶入世界。從這個意義上說，創造的起點不只是發現可能性，也是選擇追求其中某一種可能。

## 1.2 Giving Form｜賦予形式

### 英文版

We can imagine an entire world without bringing a single new thing into existence. A bridge may stand in our thoughts without crossing a river, and a melody may remain unheard by anyone else. Imagination lets us consider possibilities without requiring the world to change. Yet when we wish to build the bridge or share the music, thought alone is no longer enough. **Creation is the passage from what we can imagine to something that can exist beyond our imagination.** It begins when we act upon a possibility and find a way for it to take form in reality.

Creation does not always add new objects to the world. Sometimes it gives new form to what is already there, allowing possibilities that did not previously exist to emerge. A musician can arrange familiar notes into a composition no one has heard before. A community can transform an unused room into a place for learning without constructing another building. People may also discover new ways of cooperating by bringing existing skills and knowledge into a different relationship. In each case, the materials or abilities may already exist, while the form through which they become meaningful is new.

An idea becomes part of the world when others can encounter it without having to enter the mind that first imagined it. A book may be read by someone who never meets its author, and music may be performed by people who never heard the composer explain it. A mathematical proof can be examined independently of the person who first developed it. These works differ in form, but each makes an idea available for experience, understanding, or participation beyond its origin. Creation gives thought a presence through which others can encounter something that once belonged to one person's imagination.

The world does not simply receive what we imagine. In every act of creation, reality has something to say about what can take form. A designer may imagine a delicate structure, only to discover that its materials cannot support the intended shape. A writer may struggle to express an experience because the available words do not yet convey what needs to be said. People attempting a new way of working together may encounter differences in understanding that no original plan could fully anticipate. Such encounters are not merely interruptions to a plan. They become part of the work through which an idea acquires a form capable of existing beyond the imagination.

We do not always know what we are capable of creating until we begin. The work itself can reveal possibilities that imagination had not yet discovered. A painter may uncover an unexpected relationship between colors while working on a canvas. A composer may hear a direction in a performance that no earlier plan contained. People developing something together may arrive at an understanding that none of them possessed before the work began. The creative act can therefore become a way of learning, not simply a means of carrying out what has already been decided. Sometimes we discover what we are making through the experience of making it.

What we create can acquire a place in the world that our imagination alone could never give it. But a creation need not become a permanent object in order to be real. A musical performance becomes real through the act of playing. When the sound fades, the performance has ended, but its passing does not make it any less real. A shared practice may likewise continue only while people sustain it through their participation. Even a physical creation may depend on use and care to fulfill the purpose for which it was made. Creation therefore has no single form of existence. What makes it real is not necessarily permanence, but the way a possibility becomes available to experience, action, or participation.

### 繁體中文版

我們可以在思想中想像一整個世界，卻未必使任何新的事物真正存在。一座橋樑可以存在於腦海，卻還不能跨越河流；一段旋律可以在心中響起，卻尚未被其他人聽見。想像使我們不必改變世界，也能思考各種可能。然而，當我們希望建造橋樑或與他人分享音樂，單憑思想便不再足夠。**創造，是使想像中的可能性走出思想，成為能夠在思想之外存在的事物。** 當我們開始為某種可能性採取行動，使它得以在現實中取得形式，創造便由此發生。

創造不一定向世界增加新的物件。有時，它只是使既有的事物取得新的形式，讓過去尚未存在的可能性得以出現。音樂家可以重新組合熟悉的音符，譜出從未有人聽過的作品；一個社區可以將閒置房間變成學習的場所，而不必興建另一座建築；人們也可以透過重新建立既有知識與技能之間的關係，發現新的合作方式。在這些情況中，材料或能力可能早已存在，但使它們獲得新意義的形式，卻是新的。

當他人不必進入最初想像者的心中，也能接觸一個思想所形成的事物，它便開始成為世界的一部分。一本書可以由從未見過作者的人閱讀，一首樂曲可以由未曾聽作曲者親自解說的人演奏，一項數學證明也可以由其他人獨立檢驗。這些作品的形式各不相同，卻都使思想得以在原初的個人經驗之外，被感受、理解或參與。創造使思想取得一種可以被他人接觸的存在方式，而不再只屬於某個人的想像。

世界並不只是被動接受人的想像。在每一次創造之中，現實都會以自身的條件，決定什麼能夠取得形式。設計者可能想像一座精巧的結構，卻發現材料無法支撐預定的形態；作家可能努力表達某段經驗，卻發現既有的語言尚不足以傳達所想表達的內容；嘗試建立新合作方式的人們，也可能遇見最初的計畫無法充分預見的理解差異。這些遭遇不只是計畫受到的干擾，也成為思想逐漸取得現實形式的創造過程的一部分。

在真正開始之前，我們未必知道自己究竟能創造什麼。創造的過程本身，也可能揭示想像尚未發現的可能性。畫家可能在畫布上發現色彩之間未曾預料的關係；作曲家可能在演奏中聽見某種新的方向，而那並不存在於最初的計畫裡；共同創造的人們，也可能逐漸形成任何人在開始時都尚未具備的理解。因此，創造不只是執行已經決定的事情，也可以成為學習與發現的方法。有時，我們正是在創造之中，才逐漸認識自己究竟正在創造什麼。

我們所創造的事物，可以在世界中取得單靠想像無法賦予的位置。但一項創造不必成為永久存在的物件，才能真正存在。一場音樂演出，透過演奏而真實發生。當聲音消散，演出便已結束，但它的短暫並不減少其真實性。一種共同的實踐方式，也可能只有在人們持續參與時才得以延續。即使具有實體形態的造物，也可能需要透過使用與維護，才能實現它被創造時的目的。因此，創造並沒有唯一的存在形式。使一種可能性成為現實的，不一定是它能否永久留存，而是它如何成為人可以經驗、行動或參與的事物。

## 1.3 A World Not Ours Alone｜世界不只屬於我們

### 英文版

We never create in a world that belongs to us alone. Before our work begins, other people already inhabit the places where it will take shape, carrying memories, commitments, and aspirations of their own. A new building enters a neighborhood with familiar paths and meeting places. A new system enters a community where ways of working and communicating have developed over time. **We create within a world we share, so the significance of what we make cannot belong to its maker alone.** To understand our creations, we must recognize the lives and relationships into which they enter.

The same creation can hold different meanings for different people, because what something makes possible depends in part on the lives of those who encounter it. To an engineer, a bridge may represent a problem of structure and design. To someone who crosses it every morning, it may mean a shorter journey to work. To a family separated by the river, it may mean more time together. None of these meanings cancels the others, and no single technical description contains them all. The bridge remains the same physical structure while becoming part of several different human experiences.

What we make can become part of the conditions in which others pursue aspirations of their own. A creation's significance can grow through lives that are not extensions of its maker's life. A public library may be designed to make books available, but no architect or librarian can decide what every visitor will eventually discover there. A shared space may provide room for gatherings whose purposes were never imagined when it was built. The maker can shape the conditions in which others act, without deciding what their lives should become.

Even the work we call our own often rests upon knowledge, labor, and discoveries inherited from people we may never know. A builder relies on understandings of materials developed across generations. A researcher uses instruments made possible by discoveries that came before the present investigation. A writer works through a language shaped by countless voices and experiences. These inheritances do not make the builder's, researcher's, or writer's contribution less original. They reveal the conditions that make originality possible. No creator begins entirely alone, however personal the act of creating may feel.

A maker may know why something was created without possessing the only meaningful understanding of it. A designer can describe a chair's intended proportions, but the person who sits in it throughout a long working day knows something else about comfort and fatigue. A teacher may discover qualities in a learning tool that its developer has never directly experienced. The different perspectives are not necessarily competing explanations. They arise because people encounter the same creation through different activities and needs. The experience of others can therefore reveal aspects of a creation that the maker's understanding alone cannot provide.

Creation is not only a way of leaving something of ourselves in the world. It is also a way of joining a world already shaped by others, to which our creations can make a contribution without ever making it ours alone. The knowledge that makes our work possible may have passed through generations of people whose names we will never know. Likewise, the significance of what we make may continue through people we will never meet. A book can become part of someone's education decades after it was written; a place built for one generation can acquire a different role in the lives of another. The creator occupies only one moment within this longer continuity. Through creation, we both receive from the lives that came before us and leave possibilities for lives that will follow.

### 繁體中文版

我們從不在一個只屬於自己的世界中創造。在我們開始工作之前，其他人早已生活於造物將要進入的地方，帶著各自的記憶、牽掛與願望。一座新的建築進入某個社區時，那裡已有熟悉的道路與相聚場所；一套新的系統進入社群時，人們也早已形成自己的工作與交流方式。**我們在共同生活的世界中創造，因此，造物的意義不可能只屬於創造者自己。** 要理解我們的創造，就必須認識它將要進入的生活與關係。

同一件造物，對不同的人可以具有不同的意義，因為它使什麼成為可能，也取決於接觸它的人正在過著怎樣的生活。對工程師而言，一座橋樑可能意味著結構與設計上的難題；對每天清晨過橋的人而言，它可能意味著縮短的通勤旅程；對被河流分隔的家庭而言，它可能意味著更多相聚的時間。這些意義彼此並不排斥，也沒有任何一種技術描述能夠完整涵蓋它們。同一座橋樑保持著相同的實體形態，卻能成為不同人生經驗的一部分。

我們的創造，可以成為他人追求自身願望的生活條件。造物的意義，也可能在那些並非創造者自身延伸的生活中逐漸形成。一座公共圖書館可能是為了使人能夠接觸書籍而設計，但建築師或圖書館員，都無法決定每一位訪客最後將在其中發現什麼。一個共享場所也可能容納建造之初從未有人想像過的活動。創造者可以塑造他人行動的條件，卻不能因此替他人決定生活的方向。

即使是被我們視為個人成就的創造，也往往建立在許多未曾相識之人的知識、勞動與發現之上。建造者依靠世代累積的材料知識，研究者使用因先前發現而得以製成的儀器，作家則透過由無數聲音與經驗共同形成的語言寫作。這些繼承並不會減少建造者、研究者或作家的原創貢獻，反而揭示了原創得以成立的條件。無論創造的經驗多麼個人化，沒有任何創造者真正從一無所有開始。

創造者可能最了解自己為什麼創造某件事物，卻未必擁有理解它的唯一視角。設計師可以說明椅子預定的比例，而長時間坐在其中工作的人，對舒適與疲勞則有另一種認識。教師也可能在教學工具中發現開發者從未親身體驗的特質。這些視角未必是彼此競爭的解釋，而是源於人們透過不同的活動與需要接觸同一件造物。因此，他人的經驗可以揭示創造者單憑自身理解無法認識的面向。

創造不只是我們在世界中留下自身印記的方式，也是我們參與一個早已由他人共同塑造的世界的方式。我們可以為它增添新的事物，卻不會因此使世界只屬於自己。支持我們創造的知識，可能經過許多世代的傳承，而那些人的名字我們永遠不會知道。同樣地，我們所創造的事物，也可能透過從未謀面的人繼續獲得意義。一本書可以在寫成數十年後成為某個人學習的一部分；一個為某個世代建造的場所，也可能在下一代人的生活中取得不同的角色。創造者只是這段漫長延續中的一個時刻。透過創造，我們既承接先前生命留下的成果，也為後來的人留下新的可能。

## 1.4 Within the Universe｜在宇宙之中

### 英文版

Human creation has carried us across oceans, over continents, through the sky, and into space. Ships, aircraft, and spacecraft allow people to act far beyond the reach of the unaided body. These achievements have transformed what exploration means, but they have never made nature irrelevant. **Human creation does not place us outside nature; it reveals how much our powers depend on a world we did not create.** Every vessel, machine, and instrument works through conditions that existed before it was made. Extending our reach is therefore not the same as escaping the universe in which we live.

The ocean reveals both the limits of human life and the possibilities found within those limits. We cannot live as creatures of the sea, yet we have learned to cross its surface and explore its depths. Water prevents us from walking across an ocean, but that same water can carry a ship. Currents and winds that once made voyages uncertain can become part of the knowledge through which sailors find their way. Underwater vehicles can descend into places where people cannot remain without protection. Such inventions allow us to enter new realms of experience without requiring the ocean to become something other than itself.

A city may appear to be a world of our own making, but the land beneath it continues to sustain life through processes that no amount of construction can simply replace. Buildings shelter us, roads connect us, and machines support the movement of people and materials. Yet food, water, air, and countless living processes remain essential to the people who inhabit these structures. Some of these conditions become difficult to notice precisely because so much of daily life takes place among things humans have built. Their invisibility does not diminish their importance. A human-made landscape remains part of the living Earth rather than a separate world resting above it.

We learned to fly not by escaping the laws of nature, but by understanding them well enough to make flight possible within them. The sky did not change its properties when people first succeeded in powered flight. What changed was our understanding of how wings, motion, engines, and air could work together. A boundary that had long constrained the human body became a place through which people could travel. This achievement shows that the body's limits do not always define the limits of human action. But the capacity to cross them depends on working more deeply with nature, not on becoming independent of it.

Our spacecraft can travel far beyond Earth, but the ability to reach another world does not mean we can reproduce the conditions that make our own lives possible. A robotic probe may operate in a place where an unprotected human being could not survive even briefly. Its journey extends our knowledge, yet depends on carefully arranged materials, energy, and physical processes. Reaching a distant planet is an extraordinary achievement, but arriving somewhere and being able to live there are different accomplishments. Exploration can reveal worlds unlike our own without making them immediate homes for human life. The farther we travel, the more clearly we may recognize the particular conditions upon which our existence depends.

The universe is not simply a frontier for our achievements. It is the reality to which we belong. We often describe exploration as though human beings stood outside the world, looking toward places waiting to be discovered. Yet our capacity to ask questions, build instruments, and understand distant worlds has itself arisen within the universe we seek to know. The telescope and the eye that looks through it, the spacecraft and the people who imagine its journey, all belong to the same physical reality. Through creation, a small part of the universe has become capable of examining regions far beyond its immediate surroundings. Our achievements do not make us separate from that reality. They reveal a remarkable way in which beings within it can come to understand their own place.

### 繁體中文版

人的創造帶領我們跨越海洋、穿行大陸、飛越天空，並進入太空。船舶、飛機與太空船，使人能夠在遠遠超出身體直接所及的地方行動。這些成就改變了探索的意義，卻從未使自然變得無關緊要。**人的創造並未使我們置身自然之外，反而揭示了我們的能力如何依存於一個並非由自己創造的世界。** 每一艘船、每一台機器、每一件儀器，都依靠早在它們被製造之前便已存在的條件運作。因此，延伸活動範圍，並不等於脫離我們所生活的宇宙。

海洋既展現人類生命的界限，也蘊藏著在這些界限之中可以實現的可能。我們無法像海洋生物一樣生活，卻學會了跨越海面，並探索海洋深處。海水使我們無法步行橫越大洋，卻也能承載船隻。曾經使航程充滿不確定性的洋流與風向，可以成為水手辨認航路所依靠的知識。水下設備則能深入人類缺乏保護便無法停留的地方。這些創造使我們能夠進入新的經驗領域，卻不要求海洋變成另一種事物。

一座城市看起來彷彿是人自行建造的世界，但它腳下的土地，仍透過無法單靠增加人工建築便能取代的自然過程維繫生命。建築為我們提供居所，道路使人們相互聯繫，機器則支持人與物資的流動。然而，食物、水、空氣，以及無數維持生命的自然過程，仍然是居住其中的人不可缺少的條件。當日常生活越來越多地發生於人造事物之間，某些自然條件反而更不容易被察覺。但不被看見，並不減少它們的重要性。人工建造的地景仍屬於充滿生命的地球，而不是獨立存在於大地之上的另一個世界。

人之所以學會飛行，不是因為擺脫了自然規律，而是因為對自然有了足夠深入的理解，得以在這些規律之中實現飛行。當人類第一次實現動力飛行，天空並未因此改變自身的性質。發生改變的，是我們對機翼、運動、引擎與空氣如何相互作用的理解。長久以來限制人體行動的天空，開始成為人可以穿越的場所。這項成就說明，身體的界限不一定就是人類行動的全部界限。但跨越它們的能力，依賴的是對自然更深入的認識與運用，而不是獲得獨立於自然之外的存在。

我們的太空船可以遠離地球，但能夠抵達另一個世界，並不意味著我們也能重現使自身生命得以存在的條件。機器探測器可以在沒有保護的人體連短暫生存都十分困難的地方運作。它的旅程拓展了我們的知識，卻仍然依賴經過精密安排的材料、能源與物理過程。抵達一顆遙遠的行星，是非凡的成就，但能夠抵達與能夠生活，是兩件不同的事情。探索可以使我們發現與地球截然不同的世界，卻不會自然使它們成為人類適合居住的家園。我們走得越遠，也可能越清楚地認識自身存在所依賴的特殊條件。

宇宙不只是等待我們開拓成就的疆域，也是我們始終身處其中的現實。我們經常描述探索，彷彿人站在世界之外，望向那些等待被發現的地方。然而，人能夠提出問題、製造儀器、理解遙遠世界的能力，本身也誕生於我們試圖認識的宇宙之中。望遠鏡與透過它觀察的眼睛、太空船與想像它航程的人，都屬於同一個物理現實。透過創造，宇宙中的一小部分存在，開始有能力探索遠超自身周圍的廣闊疆域。我們的成就並沒有使自己與宇宙分離，反而揭示了身處宇宙之中的生命，如何能夠逐漸理解自己的位置。

## 1.5 Live Well｜何以好好生活

### 英文版

We can create things of extraordinary capability without necessarily making life better. A scientific instrument may reveal previously invisible features of the universe, and an artificial system may perform tasks beyond what people can accomplish unaided. Such capabilities are genuine achievements, even before we know how they may be used. Yet describing what a creation can accomplish is different from describing the life that becomes possible through it. A powerful instrument may expand understanding without changing everyday life, while a modest invention may transform how someone works or lives. The deepest question of creation is not simply what we can bring into the world, but what kind of life our creations make possible.

Some of the most meaningful creations give people room to do something other than the work they once had to perform. A machine may shorten a demanding task from several hours to a few minutes, but the significance of that change depends partly on what happens to the time released. It may allow someone to rest, learn, care for family, or pursue an interest that once had to be set aside. It may also simply lead to more work being expected within the same day. Time saved by a machine does not automatically become time a person is free to use. Creation opens a possibility, but the arrangements of life determine whether people can actually enjoy it.

Convenience does not always become freedom. A technology can remove one difficulty while quietly introducing new demands on our time, attention, and daily lives. Instant communication makes it easier to reach one another, but it can also create an expectation that every message deserves an immediate response. Devices designed to simplify a home may require repeated adjustments, updates, or attention when something stops working. These examples reveal a difference between completing a task more easily and reducing the demands placed upon a person. A technology may improve the efficiency of an activity without giving people greater freedom over how they spend their days.

As our creations become more capable of handling complexity, people should not have to organize more of their lives around that complexity. A home may contain systems that coordinate lighting, temperature, and other ordinary functions, yet its occupants should not need to become full-time operators of those systems. Greater technical sophistication can make dependable operation possible without demanding constant human supervision. This does not require people to surrender the ability to understand important conditions, make decisions, or intervene when needed. The relationship can be designed so that the system carries much of the operational burden while people retain meaningful choice. Better technology can leave more room for living.

Living well cannot be understood through individual convenience alone. Our lives unfold through relationships with other people, other living things, and a world that does not exist solely for our benefit. A creation that gives comfort to one person may depend on the labor of others, on materials drawn from distant places, or on natural processes that sustain many forms of life. Recognizing those relationships does not mean dismissing human needs. It means understanding that the conditions of a good life are never entirely private. We live through contributions and dependencies that extend beyond what we can see from our own immediate experience.

We can create simply because something is possible, or we can choose to ask what kind of life it makes possible. The direction of creation begins to take shape in that choice. A purpose does not eliminate curiosity, imagination, or the freedom to explore without knowing the result. It gives us a way to recognize which possibilities we wish to pursue further and which we may decide to leave unrealized. Nor can a single purpose determine the right life for everyone; people will continue to have different aspirations and ways of living. What we choose to create becomes one way of expressing what we believe deserves a place in the lives we share.

### 繁體中文版

我們可以創造能力非凡的事物，卻未必因此使生活變得更好。一件科學儀器可能揭示宇宙中過去無法看見的事物，一套人工系統也可能完成單憑人自身無法實現的工作。即使我們尚未知道如何運用它們，這些能力仍是真實的成就。然而，描述一件造物能夠完成什麼，與描述它使怎樣的生活成為可能，是兩種不同的認識。能力強大的儀器可能拓展理解，卻尚未改變日常生活；一項看似簡單的發明，反而可能深刻改變某個人的工作與生活方式。創造最深層的問題，不只是我們能讓什麼事物出現在世界中，而是我們的創造使什麼樣的生活成為可能。

有些最有意義的創造，使人不必再將全部時間與力量投入過去不得不完成的工作。一台機器可能將原本需要數小時的工作縮短至幾分鐘，但這項改變的意義，部分取決於省下的時間最後如何被使用。它可能使人有機會休息、學習、照顧家人，或重新投入過去不得不放下的興趣；也可能只是使人在同一天內被期待完成更多工作。機器節省的時間，不會自動成為一個人能夠自由運用的時間。創造開啟了可能，但生活中的實際安排，才決定人能否真正享有它。

便利並不總能轉化為自由。技術可以消除某項困難，卻也可能悄悄增加對人的時間、注意力與日常生活的要求。即時通訊使人更容易彼此聯繫，卻也可能形成每一則訊息都應立即回覆的期待；原本為了簡化家庭生活而設計的設備，可能需要反覆調整、更新，或在停止正常運作時投入注意力。這些例子揭示了兩種不同的事情：一項工作變得更容易完成，與一個人承受的要求真正減少，並不必然相同。技術可以提高活動的效率，卻未必使人更有自由安排自己一天的生活。

當造物越來越有能力處理複雜事務，人的生活便不應因此更加圍繞那些複雜性運轉。一個家庭可能擁有協調照明、溫度與其他日常功能的系統，但居住其中的人不應因此成為這些系統的全職操作員。更高的技術複雜度，可以用來實現可靠的運行，而不必要求人持續監督。這並不需要人放棄理解重要狀態、作出決定，或在必要時介入的能力。人與系統之間的關係，可以使系統承擔大部分運行負擔，同時使人保有真正的選擇。更好的技術，可以為生活留下更多空間。

好好生活，不能只從個人的便利來理解。我們的生活始終發生於與他人、其他生命，以及一個並非只為我們的利益而存在的世界之間的關係之中。一項為某個人帶來舒適的創造，可能依靠其他人的勞動、來自遙遠地方的材料，以及維繫許多生命的自然過程。認識這些關係，並不意味著忽略人的需要，而是理解美好生活的條件從來不完全屬於個人。我們的生活依靠許多超出眼前經驗所能直接察覺的貢獻與依存關係。

我們可以只因為某件事有可能實現，就決定去創造；也可以選擇進一步追問，它究竟使什麼樣的生活成為可能。創造的方向，正是在這種選擇之中逐漸形成。擁有目的，並不意味著放棄好奇、想像，或在尚不知道結果時自由探索的能力。目的使我們能夠辨認哪些可能性值得繼續追求，哪些則可以選擇不予實現。任何單一目的也不能替所有人決定正確的生活方式；人始終具有不同的願望與生活追求。我們選擇創造什麼，也成為我們表達哪些事物值得存在於共同生活之中的一種方式。
