# 第二章——人的尺度

**核心問題：** 我們應以什麼尺度判斷自身創造的價值？

**獨有思想內容：**

- 能夠創造，不代表創造一定值得追求。
- Capability｜能力、Possibility｜可能性與 Value｜價值並不相同。
- Intelligence｜智能擴展可能性，Wisdom｜智慧參與方向與價值判斷。
- 人的尺度不只是效能、效率或能力大小。
- 人的尺度也不能簡化為單一的生活方式或幸福標準。
- 對創造價值的判斷需要考慮人與其他生命、自然及共同世界的關係。

**本章思想成果：** 建立人的尺度作為理解、衡量和判斷創造價值的哲學依據。

**概念邊界：** 不把尺度等同於人的能力限制；不詳細分析非預期後果或責任分配。

## 2.1 Capability Is Not Value｜能力不等於價值

> Approved & Locked v1.0｜已批准並鎖定。日期｜2026-10-15。作者鎖定稿寫入，未改句子。English words｜517（僅計六段英文正文；不含標題、中文及編輯註；縮約詞計一詞）。

### 英文版

A river flows through a landscape long before anyone builds a dam across it. Its waters sustain living things, shape the land, and support human activities along its course. People have learned to alter this flow, building dams that store water, regulate its release, and generate electricity. These structures make new forms of activity possible. Water can be stored for periods of scarcity, directed toward places where it is needed, or used to produce power. Through such creations, people gain capabilities that did not previously exist in the same form.

These capabilities can be described and tested. Engineers can measure a reservoir's storage capacity, evaluate the output of its generators, and determine whether a structure meets its design requirements under specified conditions. Such measurements are not incidental. They establish whether an intended capability has actually been realized and whether it can be relied upon. A dam that performs as designed represents a genuine achievement of knowledge and engineering. Recognizing that achievement is necessary, even though it does not settle every question about the dam.

Yet the river was not an empty channel waiting for a new purpose. Its flow was already part of the conditions in which people and other living things existed. Building a dam introduces new possibilities, but it also changes some of those conditions. Activities that become possible through the dam may coexist with activities that must adapt to its presence. The technical achievement remains real. What it means, however, cannot be understood without considering the relationships into which it has entered.

The same engineering capability may therefore have different significance in different places. A reservoir may provide an important source of water where supplies are uncertain. Elsewhere, similar storage capacity may bring fewer advantages because existing conditions and needs are different. The measurements of capacity may be comparable, while the meaning of that capacity is not. This difference arises neither from a failure of engineering nor merely from a difference in personal taste. It arises because capabilities become relevant within circumstances that are not identical.

Nor does recognizing the river's existing conditions mean that leaving it unchanged is necessarily better. Human creations may sustain life, reduce hardship, and open possibilities that natural conditions alone would not provide. They may also alter relationships and conditions that already matter. Neither preserving a river exactly as it is nor changing it simply because we can is, by itself, a sufficient measure of value. Understanding what is worthwhile requires attention to both what a creation makes possible and the world in which those possibilities take shape.

Being able to do something does not tell us whether it is worth doing. This is not an argument against greater capability. It is a reason to distinguish the questions we ask of it. We may ask what a system can do, how well it does it, and under what conditions it succeeds. We must also ask what those accomplishments mean in the world where people encounter them. The first questions help us understand capability. The last begins to reveal why capability alone cannot provide the measure of its own value.

### 繁體中文版

在水壩建成之前，河流早已流經大地。河水維繫生命、塑造土地，也支持沿途的人類活動。人們學會改變水流，建造能夠蓄水、調節放水量與產生電力的水壩。這些結構使新的活動成為可能。水可以儲存起來，以備缺水時使用；可以引導至需要的地方，也可以用來發電。透過這些創造，人們獲得了過去未曾以同樣形式存在的能力。

這些能力可以描述，也可以檢驗。工程師可以測量水庫的蓄水容量、評估發電機組的輸出，並判斷結構在指定條件下是否符合設計要求。這些測量並非無關緊要。它們使人能夠確認預期能力是否真正實現，以及是否可以可靠地運用。一座按照設計運行的水壩，代表知識與工程上的真實成就。我們必須承認這項成就，即使它尚未回答關於水壩的所有問題。

然而，河流並不是一條等待被賦予新用途的空白水道。河水的流動，原本就是人與其他生命賴以存在的條件之一。建造水壩帶來新的可能性，也改變其中一些既有條件。因水壩而成為可能的活動，可能與那些必須因水壩的存在而調整的活動同時發生。技術成就依然真實，但若不考慮它進入了哪些既有關係，我們便無法充分理解這項成就的意義。

因此，同樣的工程能力，在不同地方可能具有不同的意義。在供水不穩定的地區，水庫可能成為重要的水源；在另一個地方，相近的蓄水能力卻可能因既有條件與需要不同，而帶來較少的益處。兩者的容量數據可以比較，但這些容量所具有的意義未必相同。這種差異既不是工程失敗所造成，也不只是個人喜好的不同，而是因為能力總是在各不相同的實際情境中發揮作用。

同樣地，承認河流原有的條件，也不意味著保持河流不變就必然更好。人類的創造可以維繫生命、減輕困難，並開啟單憑自然條件無法提供的可能性；它們也可能改變原本就具有重要意義的關係與條件。無論是讓河流完全維持原狀，還是僅僅因為有能力改變它便加以改變，都不足以單獨成為價值的尺度。要理解什麼值得實現，我們必須同時關注創造使什麼成為可能，以及這些可能性在怎樣的世界中展開。

能夠做某件事，並不能告訴我們它是否值得做。這並不是反對能力增長，而是提醒我們區分對能力提出的不同問題。我們可以問一套系統能做什麼、完成得如何，以及在什麼條件下能夠成功。我們也必須追問，這些成就在人們實際接觸它們的世界中具有什麼意義。前面幾個問題幫助我們理解能力；最後這個問題則開始揭示，為什麼能力本身無法成為衡量自身價值的尺度。

## 2.2 Intelligence and Possibility｜智能與可能性

> Approved & Locked v1.0｜已批准並鎖定。日期｜2026-10-15。作者鎖定稿寫入，未改句子。English words｜598（僅計六段英文正文；不含標題、中文及編輯註；縮約詞計一詞）。

### 英文版

What we can do depends partly on what we can recognize as possible. The world may permit ways of acting that have not yet entered our understanding. We may possess the means to accomplish something without knowing how to bring them together, or accept an established way of doing things without realizing that another is available. In such cases, the limits of what we recognize are not necessarily the limits of what can be done. Intelligence changes this relationship. Through learning and reasoning, possibilities that once lay beyond consideration can become available to thought and action.

Recognizing a possibility, however, is not the same as understanding what makes it possible. An unfamiliar approach may appear promising, yet depend on conditions we have not examined. What succeeds in one situation may fail in another. Experience allows intelligence to develop beyond the recognition of patterns. Relationships can be investigated, assumptions reconsidered, and earlier conclusions revised when new information becomes available. Understanding does not remove uncertainty, but it changes how possibilities can be examined. A possibility becomes more than an appealing thought when we begin to understand the conditions under which it might be realized.

Intelligence also enables us to reason about circumstances we have not yet encountered. Experience provides more than a record of previous events. It can help us recognize relationships that may hold under different conditions and consider what might follow if those conditions change. Such reasoning does not require the future to repeat the past. It allows what has been learned to inform questions that experience alone has not yet answered. In this way, intelligence extends inquiry beyond what has already occurred, bringing possible courses of events and action within reach of consideration.

Artificial intelligence extends the scale and forms of inquiry. It can examine relationships across large bodies of data, identify patterns that are difficult to distinguish through direct observation, and assist in exploring alternatives under different assumptions. This does not mean that intelligence begins with artificial systems. Human experience, reasoning, and accumulated knowledge remain part of how questions are formed and examined. What changes is the range of relationships and alternatives that can be investigated, including some that would be difficult to examine individually. Artificial intelligence thus adds new capabilities to inquiry without becoming the source of every question or possibility it helps reveal.

There is a difference between recognizing a possibility and establishing the conditions needed to realize it. An approach may become apparent before anyone has the means to carry it out. At other times, new capabilities make actions feasible that previously lay beyond practical reach. Learning and reasoning can contribute to both developments, but recognition alone does not establish the means for action. The distinction matters because intelligence may change what can be considered before anything has changed in what can actually be done. When knowledge and action eventually alter practical conditions, new possibilities may emerge in the world as well as in our understanding of it.

As understanding develops, the range of possibilities available for consideration does not merely grow. Some alternatives become clearer; others lose their apparent promise when their conditions are examined. New relationships may be recognized, and familiar approaches may acquire uses that were not previously considered. Intelligence thus changes the field of possible action, not simply by adding options, but by altering what can be understood about them. Yet even a fuller understanding leaves something unresolved. Several courses of action may remain possible, and their conditions may be understood with considerable precision. That understanding alone does not establish which course is worth pursuing. Possibility is not direction.

### 繁體中文版

我們能做什麼，部分取決於我們能夠認識哪些可能性。世界可能容許某些行動方式，只是它們尚未進入我們的理解。我們可能已具備完成某件事所需的條件，卻不知道如何將這些條件結合起來；也可能習慣於既有的做法，而沒有察覺其他途徑的存在。在這些情況下，我們認識的界限，並不必然就是行動所能達到的界限。智能改變了這種關係。透過學習與推理，原本不在考慮範圍內的可能性，得以進入思想與行動。

然而，辨認一種可能性，並不等於理解使它得以成立的條件。一種陌生的途徑可能看似可行，卻依賴我們尚未檢驗的條件。在某種情境下成功的方法，換一種情境便可能失效。經驗使智能的發展不止於辨認規律。人們可以研究事物之間的關係、重新檢視原有假設，並在取得新資訊後修正先前的結論。理解不能消除不確定性，卻能改變我們研究可能性的方式。當我們開始理解一種可能性在什麼條件下能夠實現時，它便不再只是看似可行的想法。

智能也使我們能夠推理尚未經歷的情境。經驗不只是對過去事件的記錄，也能幫助我們辨認在不同條件下可能仍然成立的關係，並思考當這些條件改變時，接下來可能發生什麼。這種推理不要求未來必須重複過去，而是讓已經獲得的認識，成為探索經驗尚未直接回答之問題的依據。如此一來，智能使探究超越已經發生的事情，讓可能的事件發展與行動途徑進入我們的考慮範圍。

人工智能擴展了探究的規模與形式。它可以研究大量資料中的相互關係，辨認難以透過直接觀察區分的規律，並協助探索不同假設下的可能途徑。這並不意味著智能始於人工系統。人的經驗、推理與累積的知識，仍然參與問題的形成與研究。真正改變的是可以被研究的關係與不同途徑的範圍，其中一些原本難以逐一考察。人工智能因此為探究增加了新的能力，卻不因此成為它所揭示之每個問題或可能性的來源。

辨認一種可能性，與建立實現它所需的條件，仍然有所不同。一條途徑可能已經進入認識，但人們尚未具備實行它的手段。有時，新的能力則使過去無法實際完成的行動變得可行。學習與推理可以參與這兩種發展，但單憑認識，並不能建立採取行動所需的條件。這項區分之所以重要，是因為智能可能先改變我們能夠考慮什麼，而實際能做的事情尚未改變。當知識與行動最終改變實踐條件時，新的可能性便可能同時出現在現實世界與我們對它的理解之中。

隨著理解不斷發展，可以納入考慮的可能性，並非只是單純增加。有些途徑變得更加清晰；另一些則在成立條件受到檢驗後，失去原先看似具有的可行性。新的關係可能被辨認，熟悉的行動方式也可能呈現過去未曾考慮的用途。因此，智能改變可能行動的範圍，不只是增加選項，也改變我們對這些選項能夠形成什麼樣的理解。然而，即使理解更加充分，仍有一個問題尚未解決。多條行動途徑可能同時可行，而我們也可能相當準確地理解它們各自的條件。單憑這種理解，仍不能確立哪一條途徑值得追求。**可能性不是方向。**

## 2.3 Wisdom and Judgment｜智慧與判斷

> Approved & Locked v1.0｜已批准並鎖定。日期｜2026-10-15。作者鎖定稿寫入，未改句子。English words｜684（僅計六段英文正文；不含標題、中文及編輯註；縮約詞計一詞）。

### 英文版

Judgment begins when we must decide what deserves our attention and action. We may have several reasons for taking a particular course, yet those reasons do not always point in the same direction. Some arise from immediate needs, others from commitments we have made or consequences we wish to avoid. To judge is not merely to select an option from those available. It is to consider the reasons for acting, examine their significance, and determine how they should guide a decision. The difficulty lies not only in knowing what can be done, but in understanding what should carry weight when we choose.

A purpose gives action direction, but it does not explain itself. We may pursue an aim because it is familiar, because others expect it of us, or because it promises a result we desire. Each offers a reason, though none necessarily settles the matter. Judgment requires us to ask what supports a purpose and whether those reasons remain convincing when examined alongside other concerns. This inquiry need not end in a single principle that governs every decision. It calls instead for an understanding of why a particular aim matters here, under these conditions, and in relation to what else we have reason to care about.

Reasons become more difficult to weigh when legitimate concerns compete. A choice that protects one interest may limit another. A benefit gained now may carry consequences later, while an action that serves one person may alter the circumstances of others. These tensions cannot always be resolved by gathering more information, because the question is sometimes not what will happen, but how different concerns should be regarded. Judgment must distinguish between what can be compromised and what should not be surrendered, between a preference that may be reconsidered and a commitment that gives a decision its meaning. Such distinctions do not eliminate conflict. They make it possible to approach conflict without pretending that every important concern can be satisfied at once.

Wisdom gives judgment depth through experience and reflection. It is expressed not simply in holding firm to a principle, nor in adjusting to every circumstance, but in knowing why firmness or flexibility may be called for. Experience can reveal the limits of earlier decisions, while reflection helps us distinguish what was mistaken in our reasoning from what changed beyond our control. Over time, this can cultivate a sense of proportion: an ability to recognize differences in importance without reducing every concern to a common measure. Wisdom does not supply a formula for resolving competing reasons. It allows judgment to become more attentive to the particular situation without losing sight of the wider life in which that situation belongs.

Artificial intelligence can participate in this work in consequential ways. It can help identify relevant considerations, examine possible consequences, and make relationships visible that might otherwise escape attention. It can also make or recommend decisions according to learned patterns, stated objectives, and operational constraints. Yet decisions made through artificial systems depend on how questions are framed, what information is available, and which considerations are included or left aside. Their participation therefore raises a further demand on judgment: not merely whether a particular recommendation performs well, but whether the grounds on which it was produced can be examined and challenged. Responsibility does not disappear when reasoning and decision-making are shared with the systems we create.

No judgment can bring all consequences under control. A decision is made within a particular understanding of the world, while the world continues to change beyond that understanding. What follows may confirm our reasons, reveal something we overlooked, or confront us with circumstances we could not have anticipated. Judgment therefore does not end when a choice is made. Its reasons remain open to examination through what happens next. Wisdom is present in the willingness to learn without abandoning responsibility, to revise without treating every earlier commitment as meaningless, and to act without demanding certainty before every step. We give direction to our capabilities through the judgments we make, and those judgments remain part of how we continue to live with what we have chosen.

### 繁體中文版

當我們必須決定什麼值得投入注意與行動時，判斷便開始發揮作用。我們可能有多種理由採取某項行動，但這些理由未必指向相同的方向。有些來自眼前的需要，有些來自已經作出的承諾，或我們希望避免的後果。判斷不只是從現有選項中挑選一項，而是思考行動的理由、審視其重要性，並確定它們應如何引導決定。困難不僅在於知道能做什麼，更在於理解作出選擇時，哪些理由應當具有分量。

目的為行動提供方向，卻不會自行說明它為何值得追求。我們可能因為習慣、他人的期待，或某種渴望的結果而追求一項目標。這些都可以構成理由，卻不一定足以作出最後決定。判斷要求我們追問目的有何依據，以及當它與其他需要考慮的事情放在一起時，原有理由是否仍然充分。這樣的探究，不必然導向一條能夠支配所有決定的普遍原則。它更需要我們理解：為什麼某個目的在此時、此地、這些條件下具有重要性，以及它與其他同樣值得關注的事情有何關係。

當不同而且具有正當理由的考量相互競爭時，權衡便更加困難。保障一方利益的選擇，可能限制另一方的利益；眼前獲得的好處，可能帶來日後的後果；對一個人有益的行動，也可能改變其他人的處境。這些張力並不總能透過蒐集更多資訊來消除，因為問題有時不在於將會發生什麼，而在於應如何看待不同的關切。判斷需要區分哪些事情可以妥協、哪些不應輕易放棄；哪些偏好可以重新考慮、哪些承諾使決定具有意義。這些區分無法消除衝突，卻使我們能夠正視衝突，而不必假裝所有重要的事情都能同時得到滿足。

智慧透過經驗與反思，使判斷獲得深度。它不只是堅守某項原則，也不是隨著每一種情況改變立場，而是理解何時需要堅持、何時可以調整，以及其中的理由。經驗可以揭示過去決定的局限，反思則幫助我們區分：究竟是原先的推理有誤，還是情況出現了我們無法控制的變化。隨著時間累積，人可能逐漸形成一種分寸感：能夠辨認不同事情的重要程度，而不必將所有關切都化約為同一套尺度。智慧不會提供一套解決各種理由衝突的公式，卻能使判斷更加關注具體情境，同時不失去對其所處生活整體的理解。

人工智能能夠以具有實際影響的方式參與這項工作。它可以協助辨認相關考量、研究可能的後果，並使原本容易被忽略的關係進入視野。它也可以依據學習到的規律、明確設定的目標與運行限制，作出或提出決策建議。然而，人工系統作出的決策，取決於問題如何被界定、有哪些資訊可用，以及哪些考量被納入或排除。因此，它的參與對判斷提出了進一步的要求：我們不只需要考察某項建議的效果，也需要能夠檢視和質疑產生這項建議的依據。當推理與決策由人和所創造的系統共同參與時，責任並不因此消失。

沒有任何判斷能夠將所有後果置於控制之下。我們總是在對世界具有某種理解的條件下作出決定，而世界仍會在這種理解之外持續變化。後續發生的事情，可能證實原先的理由，也可能揭示被忽略的問題，或使我們面對未曾預料的情況。因此，判斷不會在選擇作出的那一刻結束。隨著後續事態發展，支撐決定的理由仍然需要接受檢驗。智慧體現在願意學習而不放棄責任、願意修正而不因此否定過去的一切承諾，也體現在不要求每一步都具有確定性，仍能有所行動。我們透過判斷為自身能力確立方向，而這些判斷也持續成為我們如何與自己的選擇共同生活的一部分。

## 2.4 The Boundaries of Human Life｜人的生活邊界

> Approved & Locked v1.0｜已批准並鎖定。日期｜2026-10-15。作者鎖定稿寫入，未改句子。English words｜694（僅計六段英文正文；不含標題、中文及編輯註；縮約詞計一詞）。
> **Source｜來源：** `CH02_2.4_The_Boundaries_of_Human_Life_v1.0_LOCKED.md`。SHA-256 `6e4fc6e5596ff8497ebc4c68664bdb2c4b40019bd1dd5432a9c452a116ba772b`。作者鎖定稿。來源為 Philosophical Prose Candidate v0.2，作者批准。

### 英文版

Human life unfolds within boundaries that are not all of the same kind. Some limitations can be overcome as knowledge and capability grow. Others arise from the conditions through which life is lived: we experience the world through a body, within time, and in relation to others. These conditions are not fixed in every detail, nor do they prescribe a single way to live. Yet they cannot be treated simply as deficiencies awaiting correction. They give human experience its particular character, even as the ways we live continue to change. To understand the boundaries of human life is to distinguish what we may overcome from what makes living possible in the first place.

Time is one of these conditions. We can measure its passage with increasing precision and accomplish more within a given period, but the time of a life is not experienced merely as a succession of equal intervals. An hour spent in anticipation differs from an hour spent in rest; time shared with another person carries a significance that cannot be established by duration alone. What has passed cannot be lived again in precisely the same way, and what lies ahead remains uncertain. The meaning of time emerges through the events, commitments, and relationships that give it shape. To have more time available is not yet to know what that time will become.

Within the time we live, attention gives experience its focus. The world offers more to notice than any person can take in at once. To attend is to become engaged with something while allowing other things to remain beyond immediate awareness. This selectiveness is not only a limitation of the mind. It is part of how an experience acquires depth, how a conversation becomes meaningful, and how understanding develops through sustained involvement. Attention cannot be multiplied simply because information becomes more abundant or easier to obtain. When its direction is continually interrupted, experience itself may become fragmented. What matters is not the amount of information that reaches us, but whether we can remain present to what we are living.

Experience is also inseparable from the body through which it takes place. We encounter the world through movement, sensation, effort, rest, and physical presence. The body is not simply an instrument directed by thought; it participates in how we learn, remember, and understand. A description of an experience may preserve knowledge about it without reproducing what it is like to live through it. This distinction does not place bodily experience beyond the reach of knowledge or technological assistance. Rather, it reminds us that information about an experience and the experience itself are not interchangeable. Human life is lived through this involvement with the world, not merely through representations of it.

No human life, however, unfolds through individual experience alone. Our lives take shape in the presence of others, whose experiences we can never wholly possess and whose choices we cannot fully determine. A relationship involves more than an exchange of information or the coordination of separate activities. It develops through the time people give one another, the ways they respond, and the freedom each retains to remain a person in their own right. Shared life therefore contains a boundary that cannot be overcome simply through greater control or closer coordination. Living with others means encountering lives that are connected with our own without becoming extensions of it. The possibility of belonging rests partly on this difference.

These boundaries do not require us to preserve every difficulty or accept every limitation as inevitable. Knowledge, care, and technology can change conditions that once confined human life, opening ways of living that were previously beyond reach. Yet the expansion of what we can do does not erase the conditions through which life is experienced. Time remains lived rather than merely measured. Attention remains something we give. The body remains part of our presence in the world, and other lives retain their own directions. These conditions leave room for many ways of living, including ways not yet imagined. They do not prescribe what a life must become. They give us a basis for recognizing that life remains more than the capabilities available within it.

### 繁體中文版

人的生活在不同性質的邊界之中展開。有些限制可以隨著知識與能力的增長而克服；另一些則來自生活本身得以展開的條件：我們透過身體、在時間之中，並在與他人的關係裡經驗世界。這些條件並非每個細節都固定不變，也不規定唯一的生活方式。然而，它們不能簡單地被視為等待修正的缺陷。即使生活方式持續改變，這些條件仍賦予人的經驗特有的性質。理解人的生活邊界，就是區分哪些限制可以克服，以及哪些條件使生活從根本上成為可能。

時間是其中一項條件。我們可以越來越精確地測量時間的流逝，也能在一定時間內完成更多事情，但生命中的時間，並不只是由一連串等長的間隔所構成。一小時的等待與一小時的休息，具有不同的經驗；與他人共同度過的時間，其意義也不能單憑長短來確定。已經過去的時刻，無法以完全相同的方式重新經歷；尚未到來的時間，則始終帶有不確定性。時間的意義，透過其中發生的事情、所承擔的承諾與所形成的關係逐漸顯現。擁有更多可以運用的時間，還不等於知道這些時間將成為什麼樣的生活。

在我們生活的時間之中，注意力使經驗具有焦點。世界中值得察覺的事物，遠多於任何人能夠在同一時刻完整接收的範圍。投入注意，意味著我們與某件事物建立當下的聯繫，同時讓其他事物暫時留在直接意識之外。這種選擇性不只是心智能力的限制，也是經驗得以深入、交談得以產生意義，以及理解透過持續參與而發展的條件。資訊即使變得更加豐富、更容易取得，注意力也不會因此成倍增加。當注意的方向不斷受到打斷，經驗本身也可能變得破碎。重要的不只是有多少資訊抵達我們面前，而是我們能否真正投入正在經歷的生活。

經驗也無法與承載經驗的身體分離。我們透過移動、感受、付出、休息與親身在場接觸世界。身體不只是受思想指揮的工具，它也參與我們學習、記憶與理解的過程。對一段經驗的描述，可以保存與它有關的知識，卻未必能重現親身經歷時的感受。這項區分並不意味著身體經驗不能成為知識與技術協助的對象，而是提醒我們：關於經驗的資訊，與經驗本身，不能彼此替代。人的生活透過這種與世界的親身相遇而展開，而不只是透過對世界的描述。

然而，人的生活從來不只透過個人的經驗展開。我們的生活在他人的存在中逐漸形成，而他人的經驗無法被我們完全占有，他們的選擇也無法由我們全然決定。一段關係不只是資訊交換，或各自行動之間的協調。它透過人們願意給予彼此的時間、相互回應的方式，以及每個人仍能作為獨立個體而生活的自由逐漸發展。因此，共同生活具有一種無法單靠更強的控制或更緊密的協調來消除的邊界。與他人共同生活，意味著我們所面對的生命雖然與自身相連，卻不會因此成為自身的延伸。歸屬的可能性，部分正建立在這種差異之上。

這些邊界並不要求我們保留每一種困難，或把所有限制都視為無可避免。知識、照顧與技術可以改變那些曾經束縛人的生活的條件，使過去難以實現的生活方式成為可能。然而，我們能做的事情即使持續擴展，也不會因此消除生活得以被經歷的基本條件。時間始終是被生活的，而不只是被測量的；注意力始終需要我們親自投入；身體仍是我們在世界中存在的一部分，而其他生命也各自擁有自己的方向。這些條件容許多種生活方式，包括尚未被想像出來的方式。它們不規定一個人的生活必須成為什麼樣子，卻使我們能夠認識到：生活始終大於其中可以運用的能力。

## 2.5 What Makes Creation Worthwhile｜什麼使創造值得

> Approved & Locked v1.0｜已批准並鎖定。日期｜2026-10-15。作者鎖定稿寫入，未改句子。English words｜659（僅計六段英文正文；不含標題、中文及編輯註；縮約詞計一詞）。
> **Source｜來源：** `CH02_2.5_What_Makes_Creation_Worthwhile_v1.0_LOCKED.md`。SHA-256 `07f85c107444476c11b85a2a5a3e976909c0b487440369c088ec40cfeacf8f4a`。作者鎖定稿。來源為 Candidate v0.3，作者批准。

### 英文版

A creation enters a world already filled with lives, purposes, and relationships. Its significance cannot be understood solely from what its makers intended or what it is capable of doing. Once it becomes part of the world, it changes the circumstances in which people act, depend on one another, and pursue their lives. Some of these changes may be anticipated; others become apparent only through use and experience. The worth of a creation therefore cannot be settled entirely at the moment it is made. It must also be understood through the life that unfolds around it.

Every creation that becomes part of life establishes conditions for its continued use. It may require care, maintenance, resources, or knowledge that must be sustained long after the work of making it is complete. Some of these requirements are foreseeable; others emerge as circumstances change and dependence grows. They are not simply costs to be minimized, for continued care can itself be an expression of what people value. What matters is whether the demands a creation introduces can be recognized, shared, and responsibly sustained. Its worth must be considered not only through what it enables, but through what must continue to be done for it to remain part of the world.

Over time, what people create can change the practices through which they live. New capabilities may become familiar, expectations may adjust, and ways of acting that once required deliberate choice may settle into habit. A creation can thus influence not only how a purpose is pursued, but what people come to regard as necessary or ordinary. Such influence can support cooperation and continuity, yet it can also make established arrangements difficult to question. To understand a creation's place in life, we must consider how it shapes expectations and whether the ways of living that develop around it remain open to reconsideration.

Nor can the worth of creation be measured by the experience of its immediate users alone. Every creation depends on conditions beyond itself: materials, energy, labor, knowledge, and a world in which its consequences must be borne. What benefits one group may place demands on others who did not participate in its design or choose to depend on it. Some consequences extend beyond human relationships into the living systems and environments that make human life possible. To create for human well-being is not to regard everything else as existing for human use. It is to recognize that the conditions of a good human life cannot be separated from the wider world in which that life takes place.

A worthwhile creation need not remain unchanged, nor must its original purpose govern every future use. Circumstances develop, knowledge improves, and needs that once seemed enduring may become less important. What matters is whether the creation can continue to be understood and cared for as these changes occur. Its presence should not make necessary change unnecessarily difficult, or place the consequences of its limitations beyond the reach of those affected. Sometimes care calls for improvement; sometimes for restraint, replacement, or an end to what has been built. The willingness to reconsider a creation is not a rejection of the effort that brought it into being. It is part of taking seriously the world into which it was introduced.

The reasons for preserving a creation need not be the same as those that first brought it into being. What it enables may change, the relationships it supports may deepen, and consequences once beyond consideration may become impossible to ignore. Its value therefore calls for continuing attention to what it contributes, what it requires, and how its presence affects the lives and conditions around it. This does not mean that every creation must prove its worth anew at every moment. It means that no initial achievement can exempt it from reconsideration as the world changes. Creation becomes worthwhile not simply through what has been accomplished, but through the life it helps make possible over time.

### 繁體中文版

一項創造進入的，是一個早已存在生命、目的與各種關係的世界。它的意義，不能只從創造者的意圖或它所具備的能力來理解。一旦進入世界，它便開始改變人們行動、相互依賴及展開生活的條件。其中一些改變可以事先預見，另一些則只有在實際使用與經驗中才逐漸顯現。因此，一項創造是否值得，不能在它完成的那一刻便得到全部答案。我們還需要從它所參與的生活中，理解它的意義。

每一項進入生活的創造，都會形成使其得以持續使用的條件。它可能需要照顧、維護、資源或知識，而這些投入往往在創造工作完成後仍須長期維持。有些要求可以預先了解，另一些則隨著情況改變與依賴加深而逐漸出現。它們不只是需要盡可能降低的成本，因為持續照顧一項事物，本身也可能表達人們對它的重視。重要的是，一項創造所帶來的要求，能否被認識、共同承擔，並以負責任的方式持續維持。理解它是否值得，不只需要考察它使什麼成為可能，也需要考察為了讓它繼續存在於世界之中，人們必須持續做些什麼。

隨著時間推移，人們所創造的事物，可能改變日常生活的實踐方式。新的能力逐漸變得熟悉，期待隨之調整，原本需要有意識選擇的行動方式，也可能慢慢成為習慣。因此，一項創造不只影響人們如何追求某個目的，也可能改變人們認為什麼是必要或理所當然的。這種影響可以支持合作，使生活具有延續性，卻也可能讓既有安排變得難以質疑。要理解一項創造在生活中的位置，我們必須考察它如何塑造期待，以及圍繞它形成的生活方式，是否仍然容許重新審視。

創造的價值，也不能只由直接使用者的經驗來衡量。每一項創造都依賴自身之外的條件：材料、能源、勞動、知識，以及一個必須承受其後果的世界。對某些群體有益的事物，可能對另一些沒有參與設計、也未曾選擇依賴它的人提出要求。有些後果更會超越人際關係，進入使人類生活成為可能的生命系統與自然環境。為人的美好生活而創造，並不意味著世界上的其他事物都只為人的使用而存在。它意味著我們必須認識到，良好的人類生活所依賴的條件，無法與生活所在的更廣闊世界分離。

一項值得存在的創造，不必始終保持原樣，也不需要讓最初的目的支配所有未來的用途。環境會變化，知識會進步，原本看似長久的需要，也可能不再那麼重要。關鍵在於，當這些變化發生時，人們能否繼續理解並妥善照顧這項創造。它的存在不應使必要的改變變得不必要地困難，也不應讓受其局限影響的人無從應對那些後果。有時，妥善對待一項創造意味著改進；有時則意味著節制、替換，或讓已經建成的事物走向終結。願意重新審視一項創造，並不是否定曾經投入其中的努力，而是認真對待它所進入的世界。

決定繼續保留一項創造的理由，不必然等同於當初使它誕生的理由。它所能實現的事情可能改變，它所支持的關係可能逐漸深化，而過去未曾充分考慮的後果，也可能變得無法忽視。因此，理解它的價值，需要持續關注它帶來什麼、要求什麼，以及它的存在如何影響周圍的生命與條件。這並不意味著每一項創造都必須時時刻刻重新證明自身的價值，而是說，任何最初的成就，都不能使它在世界改變時免於重新審視。創造之所以值得，不只因為已經完成了什麼，也在於它隨著時間推移，幫助什麼樣的生活成為可能。
